# Coming-soon story film: Sita's necklace

Status: planned, not generated. Higgsfield balance is 0 credits (free plan); generation waits for credits.

## Story and sources

- Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda (sarga 128 in critical/Gita Press numbering, ch. 131 in Shastri's translation): Rama gives Sita a pearl necklace; she looks from the vanaras to Rama; Rama tells her to give it to whom she is pleased with; she gives it to Hanuman, in whom steadfastness, humility, wisdom and courage always dwell.
- Later popular retelling (not in Valmiki; popularised in Bhakti tradition and Amar Chitra Katha): Hanuman opens the pearls looking for Rama, says what does not hold Rama is worthless to him, and opens his heart, where Rama and Sita are seen.
- Depiction choices: Sita makes the choice; the heart opens as light, never a wound; devotional, never comic.

| S2 Choice | Sita lifts the necklace off and looks to Rama; he opens his palm to her: the choice is hers. | Rama | Give it to the one you love most. |
| S3 Gift | Sita lays the pearls in Hanuman's cupped hands; he bows his head. | Sita | For you, Hanuman. |
| S4 Search (close-up) | Hanuman opens one pearl like a tiny shell and peers inside: empty. | From the court | He breaks the queen's gift? |
| S5 Answer | Hanuman places his hand over his heart and looks up to Rama, calm. | Hanuman | Without Rama, it is nothing to me. |
| S6 Heart (close-up) | He draws his hands apart; a lotus of warm gold light blooms at his chest, Rama and Sita seated within. | Hanuman | Look. They are here. |

Act I (S1–S3) plays while the light threads the mala and ends as the real knot is tied. Act II (S4–S6) plays while the beads return to the light and carries across the loop into the next descent. The wide rest view shows only a bridging caption.

## Art direction

**Style:** Chandan Ink: sepia brush-line devotional animation on ivory paper

**Style block (append to every prompt):**

> Hand-inked 2D animation that looks like a classical Indian devotional painting come to life. Costume, jewellery and iconography follow Raja Ravi Varma closely. Everything is drawn as refined sepia brush-pen line art with soft, transparent watercolour washes on warm ivory handmade paper (#efe6d8). The mood is calm, reverent and timeless: a quiet luxury editorial film, not a calendar print. Acting is gently anime-influenced: clear readable silhouettes, expressive almond eyes with soft highlights, graceful hand gestures (abhaya and anjali mudras, open offering palms), characters turning toward each other, meeting eye-lines and speaking softly with small mouth movements. Silk, hair, tassels and flower garlands move a little behind each action. Every movement is slow and eased, with held poses of about half a second. Line: one tapered brush contour in dark sepia (#3b2a1e, never pure black), slightly heavier on the shadow side, with thin interior lines for jewellery and folds. Shading: only two or three translucent wash tones, lit softly from the upper left, with a little granulation and edges that bleed lightly past the line. The palette is strictly limited to sepia ink, walnut brown (#6b4a32), deep rakta red (#8e2a1c), muted antique gold (#b08d57), and warm wheat and ochre skin washes. The only cool tone is a soft indigo-slate wash, used only for Rama's skin. Figures are isolated near the centre of a completely plain, empty ivory paper background, with wide empty margins on every side. There is no setting and no ground line, only a faint pale wash shadow under the figures. The film is one continuous shot with a static or very slow, gentle camera, cinematic 24 fps timing, and museum-quality draughtsmanship.
